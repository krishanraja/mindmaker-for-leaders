## Q1. The first five seconds (morning open)  · intensity 3/5
*Scene:* It's 7:42am. A time-poor CEO who barely reads the news opens CTRL one-handed on the train. Before they tap anything, here's what the screen could greet them with.
**Ranking (best→worst):**
1. **D · The digest** — A crisp, scannable 3-item digest: the top 3 AI/market signals relevant to their business, readable in 15 seconds, each tappable for more.
2. **C · The living decision** — Their own decision from last week, resurfaced because something changed: 'One assumption behind your hiring call just shifted. Want to re-examine it?'
3. **B · The open prompt** — A warm, open question: 'Morning, Sarah. What's the biggest decision on your mind today?' — the app hands them the wheel and waits.
4. **A · The synthesized signal** — One beautiful, already-made visual card: 'Here's the single thing moving against your move upmarket this morning' — a fresh real-world signal tied to a priority they told you mattered, with one clear move beneath it.
**Why:** The open prompt is a really vague open question that most busy people will just get stressed by, rather than enjoy. But it does encourage fast data collection which I like. I just think open ended questions that force the user to think are not the way forward. Presenting them with options to choose from, and those options being as personalized as possible - that is a much better call. The Living Decision is a pretty good idea because it highlights how quickly things move and can probably be turned into the chronology or the time lapse of a decision based on everything that changes around it. That's obviously super complex, but I like that idea. The digest is probably, in one hand, the most easy to consume based on current consumer behavior. Everyone knows what a digest is. Everyone reads newsletters and has them in their inbox, so this could just be positioned as a better version of that. And the briefing ties into the Digest probably the most closely. The synthesized signal reads like the best one, but in practical reality, I can't imagine us getting that kind of thing 100% correct enough of the time that it would be worth that real estate. We have to presume that there's not going to be as much data as we want in the app.

## Q9. Handing work off + the reinvestment moment  · intensity 5/5
*Scene:* You just automated something that used to eat your time. What should happen next?
**Ranking (best→worst):**
1. **A · Offer the next rung** — 'That's handled — now point the time you freed at amplifying [a strength].'
2. **D · Nudge later** — A day later: 'Your automation ran twice — want to aim that saved time at your big bet?'
3. **B · Bank the reclaim** — 'You just saved ~3 hrs/week' — celebrated and left there.
4. **C · Silent** — It quietly files the automation and says nothing; you move on.
**Why:** We need to be really clear as to what 'automated', means. Right now, that's just words. We need to tie that into action, which is what the product actually does.

Right Now, in the simplest form, the app builds skills and personalized markdown files that can be used to train any tool that they're using. I don't want this app to be where they do their content writing, or their board memos, or their decks. That would make this app too complicated. This app is purely designed to help them level up their AI usage week over week so that they form the right habits to use it and also to save time to invest in sharper skills. I'm quite confused on the vision for this one, because in reality, we can't have an app that is just there to make custom claude skills that they have to copy paste manually - thats no long term value. But at the same time, it is the most tangible way to have a leader see their own skills sharpen and  improve the skills they will need when AI has automated all their boring/administrative/hated tasks away. I think this entire concept probably needs to be rethought end to end as it feels like a completely different track from the other thesis, which is surfacing the right external signals + user signals to curate whats important so the leader can think critically and use the tool to explore and ideate in private. Perhaps this entire concept moves in to the Kit area, and the app forks more cleanly than is does right now? This piece is more about the small wins day to day actually getting better at using AI for little outputs, which is where my cohort students need to go. it could also be a pretty great data collection for the other fork
