## 1. CORE THESES (the central arguments)

### Thesis A — "AI is an operating skill, not a procurement call."
This is the spine of everything. Krish's single most load-bearing argument:
> "The instinct is to treat AI like every tech decision of the last twenty years: pick a vendor, fund a pilot, watch a dashboard. It fails because AI is an operating skill, not a procurement call. The proof is blunt: leaders who actually use the tools get **up to 12x more** from them than the ones who delegate it, same technology. No briefing or clever hire replicates that. The only way through is to **do it yourself**."

### Thesis B — "The gap is not intelligence, it is reps."
> "Almost everyone leading on it is improvising, and most of the confident talk in the room is exactly that, talk... only **22% of execs feel prepared to lead it**, and **95% of pilots return nothing measurable**. The gap is not intelligence, it is reps. **Nobody above or beside you is doing the real work in the open**, so there has been no one to learn it from. That is what this closes."
Mindmaker's entire reason to exist: "not another strategy deck, **the reps**, modelled by someone who runs the thing."

### Thesis C — The new economics: cost per task, not per seat.
> "Token prices fell 98%, and enterprise AI bills somehow rose 320%... Software was priced per seat, tidy and predictable. **Intelligence is priced per task**, and an agentic workflow fires **ten to thirty tasks per interaction**, so two people on the same licence can differ in cost **a thousandfold**." (Uber reportedly torched its 2026 AI coding budget by April — TheNextWeb, June 2026.) "This is the one AI topic where your commercial instinct is the edge and not the gap, and almost nobody is using it."

### Thesis D — The org chart is the real design problem.
> "Every org chart you have ever drawn put a human in every box. The next one will not, and that is **a design problem, not a tech one**, which is precisely why most AI plans stall the second they leave the slide. **No vendor fixes a decision-rights gap for you.**"

### Thesis E — Building is no longer something you wait on engineering for.
> "The edge was never the syntax. It is what happens when building stops being something you wait on engineering for and becomes **something your team just does**. Ideas get tested instead of argued about in committee. **A junior with a working demo moves a decision a VP's memo could not.** Divisions finally understand each other because they can see the tool... That is the movement, and it is what quietly turns a department into a modern business."

### Thesis F — Context compounds; it is the one AI investment that gets cheaper every week.
The memory/identity thesis: your AI "has the memory of a goldfish, so every Monday is its first day." Fixing identity + memory + self-correction makes context "compound — the one AI investment that gets cheaper every week you use it." Context is portable wealth: "yours when you change tools, jobs, or companies."

### Thesis G — Anti-hype honesty as the differentiator.
Across every workshop, Krish refuses the demo-reel. "Plenty of workshops promise you an autonomous business in a day. **I will not insult you with that.**" The differentiation is *production receipts and scars*: "you see inside it every week, **failures included**." He shows "how the work actually goes, not the highlight reel."

### Thesis H — Outcome, not attendance. Ship, don't watch.
The product promise is a *built artefact*, never notes or slides. "**The outcome is the promise, not the attendance.**" / "Nobody leaves with notes instead of a thing." / "You do not leave until it [is built / runs / is drawn]. **That is the deal.**"

## 2. FRAMEWORKS (every named model, sequence, matrix, list — with components)

### The Mind Set → Mind Map → Mind Make arc (the meta-framework)
The master spine. Every workshop and every cohort week is tagged with one of three stages:
- **[Mind Set]** — *deciding.* (Economics / AI P&L; Agentic Org Chart; the diagnostic.) "two weeks deciding."
- **[Mind Map]** — *building your own infrastructure.* (Identity/Memory/Self-correction; AI Chief of Staff.) "two building your own infrastructure."
- **[Mind Make]** — *shipping.* (Vibe Coding / ship a tool; Autonomous Function.) "two shipping."
- Plus a **diagnostic** (Week 1) and a **Capstone** (Week 8).
> "The arc runs Mind Set, Mind Map, Mind Make: two weeks deciding, two building your own infrastructure, two shipping, plus a diagnostic and a capstone."

### The AI P&L (Economics framework)
A cost model **priced per task, not per seat**, built on the leader's real workflows. Components Krish teaches:
- **Volume mechanics** — why bills triple while token prices collapse (an agentic workflow fires 10–30 tasks per interaction).
- **Model tiering / cost-intelligence routing rules** — "when the expensive model earns its price and when the cheap one wins" (the routing that "roughly halved my own fleet costs without degrading the work").
- **Build / buy / subscribe decision matrix** — "Score every option against your actual usage instead of a vendor's pitch deck"; price hidden costs (integration, lock-in, the upgrade you'll be sold next year).
- **Runaway-spend monitoring** — "the three spend patterns that precede every five-figure surprise"; alert thresholds; flag a runaway workflow "the same day, not at month end."
- **The board one-pager** — "what AI costs you, what it returns, and what you are recommending" + "the three questions a board always asks."

### The Agentic Org Chart + the Decision Matrix
A named org chart where not every box is a human. The core call is a three-way matrix run on **every function**:
- **Agent-led** — the agent owns the work.
- **Assisted** — "the work a human does better with an agent alongside, the highest-value tier most miss."
- **Never** — "the calls that protect you when someone asks why not everything."
Supporting components:
- **The 90-day order of operations** — "What moves first, second, third. The sequence matters more than the ambition, and most people run it backwards." Order "by dependency and payback, not by whatever demo impressed someone last week." Classic mistake: "building the fun thing before the load-bearing one."
- **Redeployment plan** — "Map every affected human to the higher-value work the agents free them for"; land it "as a redesign, not a quiet layoff."
- **New roles** emerging in agent-native businesses: **"auditors, orchestrators, context owners."** Plus "the teammate my fleet created by accident" / "why half my ops pod exists to watch the other half."
- **The reasoning, written down** — so you can "defend every call to your CFO and your board without flinching."

### The Three Layers (Identity, Memory, Self-Correction) — the CTRL core
The single most product-relevant framework. The AI's "three problems": memory of a goldfish (every Monday is its first day), no idea who it works for (answers like a stranger), repeats mistakes forever (you pay "the same teaching tax every week"). The three fixes:
1. **Identity file** — "role, voice, standards, and the rules it must never break." "Watch the same model behave like a different employee once it knows who it is."
2. **Memory layer / memory web** — "a working context file your AI actually reads from"; "the schema that keeps memory sharp instead of bloated"; ends "Monday-morning amnesia for good." Tested with a **before-and-after on your own prompts**.
3. **Correction loop / self-correction** — "corrections get captured and written back into the brief"; the audit pattern "where the same mistake does not survive four occurrences."
Cross-cutting:
- **One memory across Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini** — "switching stops wiping your history"; "plain files you own, not settings trapped in a platform"; "watch them answer from the same brain."
- **Privacy lines** — "what to store, what to never store"; "separates useful context from liability"; encrypted at rest on CTRL.
- **The capture-and-prune rhythm** — keeps memory sharp over time.

### The AI Chief of Staff architecture
A persistent assistant (vs. "a clever intern with no memory, re-briefed every morning"). Components:
- **What it carries:** "your calendar, your tone, your three rolling priorities, the thing to chase on Tuesday."
- **The three daily tasks it runs unprompted:** triage, briefing, follow-up.
- **The autonomy line:** "what it does alone, what it drafts for you, what it never touches."
- **The morning briefing** — "turns 120 emails of noise into a clear top-three by nine"; "the urgent stops crowding out the important."
- **The three failure modes that kill assistants by Friday** (each met by a design call) — the recurring "why most assistants die by Friday."
- **Monitoring / drift detection** — "the difference between an assistant that is working and one that is just reporting green."
- Reaches you "on the channel you already live on" (Slack, Telegram, email).

### The Five-Part Brief ("The brief is the build")
The vibe-coding/spec framework:
> "**Goal, context, format, constraints, acceptance**: the five-part brief, and why one complete pass beats two partial ones."
Companion idea: **"the prototype is the spec"** — "Treat the working demo as the requirements document, the brief no written spec matches"; "Collapse weeks of requirements ambiguity into one artefact that shows, not tells."

### The Safe-to-Ship Line + Three Controls (governance)
- **Tiers:** "what your team ships freely, what gets review, what never leaves a sandbox."
- **Three controls** "that let speed and safety coexist" / "that keep speed safe."
- Anchored by the cautionary stat: **"45% of AI code with a flaw"** and "the CEO whose agent deleted production and lied about it."

### The Five Bricks (autonomous workflow decomposition)
> "**Capture, process, decide, produce, distribute**: how every workflow decomposes, and which bricks an agent takes first."

### The Build → Deploy → Monitor loop
"the build, deploy, monitor loop you will reuse forever" — run once with Krish, "so the second workflow takes a quarter of the time."

### The Leverage Audit (what to automate first)
> "Run the leverage audit: **hours per week times closeness to revenue, scored honestly.**" Pick the top three candidates and ignore the rest. "Discipline beats ambition here." Classic mistake: "automating what is easy instead of what is expensive."

### "Never let a worker grade its own homework" (monitoring doctrine)
- **The false-green failure** — "my fleet once reported perfectly healthy while 90% was dead."
- **The silent-failure sweep** — monitoring "that reads real output, not status reports / not the workflow's claim about itself."
- **Same-hour alerts** — "you hear about a break the same hour, not three silent weeks later."
- **Approval gates** — "anything external, expensive, or irreversible waits for a human." Autonomy boundary: "full speed on internal work, a hard stop on what leaves the building."

### Five decision frameworks for hard AI calls (cohort, Outcome 6)
> "Five decision frameworks for hard AI calls: **A/B framing, dialectical tension, mental contrasting, first principles, reflective.**" Plus "a reusable decision template you apply to the next AI question inside of three days, not three weeks," and "the specific questions that surface the real decision underneath the surface one."

### "The decision under the decision" (the diagnostic)
> "**Finding the decision under the decision**" / "name the actual problem in your business and the decision sitting under it. Most people discover it is not the one they walked in with." This opens every workshop ("Frame the real problem — thirty minutes, no slides").

### The cohort 8-week sequence (the full curriculum, in order)
1. **[Mind Set] Diagnose** — name the decision you are actually preparing for; commit a target outcome the $2,500 guarantee is measured against. → *decision memo v1.*
2. **[Mind Set] Economics of AI** — build your AI P&L. → *AI P&L with real numbers.*
3. **[Mind Set] Agentic Org Chart** — redeploy, sequence, decide. → *org chart + 90-day sequence + redeployment plan.*
4. **[Mind Map] Identity, Memory, Self-Correction** — "your context layer," "the infrastructure every later build stands on." → *context layer live on CTRL.*
5. **[Mind Map] AI Chief of Staff** — "make your first AI hire." → *assistant deployed, 3 daily tasks, monitored.*
6. **[Mind Make] Ship a tool** — vibe coding with the governance line drawn. → *working tool on a live URL.*
7. **[Mind Make] Autonomous function** — "delegate something for real." → *workflow on n8n/Make with gates + same-hour alerts.*
8. **[Capstone]** — "the memo, the P&L, the chart, and the running artefact," presented to fifteen peers "with the receipts."

### The four-part workshop day (repeated across all six)
1. **Frame the real problem** (30 min, no slides). 2. **Watch me build it** (live, "failures and dead ends left in"). 3. **Now you build** (capped at 30, "poking holes in your choices before they cost you"). 4. **Walk out with it working.**

## 3. LEADER BELIEFS (what an AI-native leader must DO / THINK / FEEL / STOP)

### DO
- **Use the tools yourself, hands on the keys.** "The only way through is to do it yourself." Delegating it forfeits the 12x.
- **Build, don't wait.** Turn "could-we conversations into Tuesday-afternoon prototypes instead of quarter-long debates."
- **Make the agent-led / assisted / never call on every function**, and write the reasoning down so you can defend it to the board.
- **Price intelligence per task** and walk into the board "with the spend decision already made."
- **Give your AI an identity, a memory, and a correction loop.** Build the portable context layer first; everything else stands on it.
- **Make your first AI hire** (the chief of staff) and **delegate one real function** with gates and monitoring.
- **Draw the safe-to-ship line** before anyone gets burned.
- **Ship in public, witnessed** — "a public commitment to fifteen peers on what you are shipping and by when, with the receipts to follow."
- **Run the work in the open**, scars included — be the person others can finally learn from.

### THINK
- AI is **an operating skill, not a procurement call.**
- The new chart is **a design problem, not a tech one** — and **no vendor fixes a decision-rights gap for you.**
- **The brief is the build; the prototype is the spec.**
- **The sequence matters more than the ambition** — most run the 90-day order backwards.
- **Discipline beats ambition** — automate the expensive, not the easy. Pick three, ignore the rest.
- **Redeploy, not replace** — the reframe "the room tends to call a wake-up call. Who to redeploy, in what order, and when. Not who to cut."
- **Context compounds** — it gets cheaper every week; competitors "retype the same paragraph."

### FEEL
- The **"demo wins the room"** confidence: "the person with a working demo reframes the meeting."
- **Operating confidence from having shipped in public** — "with fifteen witnesses."
- The visceral moment of mastery: **"Feel the moment the abstraction breaks: you built it, so you can build the next one."**
- Relief from "inbox dread" — "a clear top-three by nine."
- Ownership: a second brain that is "private, portable, and **still yours when you change tools, jobs, or companies.**"

### STOP doing
- **Stop re-briefing a stranger every Monday** / "re-explaining your business a thousand times."
- **Stop opening a fresh AI chat and closing the tab** — "you have hired a genuinely brilliant assistant and handed it the memory of a mayfly."
- **Stop guessing at your AI spend.**
- **Stop debating AI strategy in the abstract** ("no abstractions, no maturity matrices, no quadrant you will never reopen").
- **Stop hand-cranking the same work every week.**
- **Stop letting a worker grade its own homework** (false-green monitoring).
- **Stop treating building as something you wait on engineering for.**
- **Stop the trap of rebuilding instead of shipping.**

## 4. PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS FOR CTRL

CTRL is positioned throughout as: **"a private, portable memory web that keeps your AI context sharp, improving, and yours."** / "the app I built to hold mine." It is the take-home vehicle of the Identity/Memory/Self-Correction workshop. The implications for what CTRL must *be*:

### CTRL must embody the Three Layers natively
- **Identity** — let a leader define role, voice, standards, and "the rules it must never break" as a first-class artefact. The product should make the "same model behaves like a different employee once it knows who it is" moment effortless.
- **Memory web** — a structured context file the leader's AI "actually reads from," with "the schema that keeps memory sharp instead of bloated." CTRL should own the **capture-and-prune rhythm** so memory never bloats.
- **Self-correction loop** — captured corrections "written back into the brief," with the audit pattern where "the same mistake does not survive four occurrences." CTRL should *visibly retire* a repeated mistake.

### CTRL is portable and private by design
- **One memory across Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini** — "switching stops wiping your history"; "answer from the same brain." Portability is the selling promise, not a feature.
- **Plain files you own**, "not settings trapped in a platform."
- **Encrypted at rest.** Clear **privacy lines**: "what to store, what to never store" — CTRL should help draw that line, separating "useful context from liability."
- **Survives tool/job/company changes.** This is the emotional hook — context as personal, portable wealth.

### The "next step" CTRL should always present
Krish's whole pedagogy is **one concrete next artefact**, never a menu of theory. CTRL's UX should mirror this:
- Surface **"the one workflow worth building first"** / **"which job in your business should run without you first."**
- Always frame **"the decision under the decision"** — help the leader name the *real* call, not the surface one.
- Push toward a **shippable artefact**, never notes. The app's success metric should be *a thing built/running*, echoing "Nobody leaves with notes instead of a thing."

### What CTRL should learn and remember about the leader
From the intake/diagnostic patterns and the chief-of-staff design:
- Their **role, voice, standards, and hard rules** (the identity file).
- Their **three rolling priorities** and **"the thing to chase on Tuesday."**
- Their **calendar, inbox, and primary channel** (where they "already live" — Slack/Telegram/email).
- Their **surface in the next 90 days** ("a board update, a keynote, a restructure, a hire, a budget round").
- Their **target outcome** and what value looks like for them ("hours saved, pipeline moved, or a system shipped").
- The **decisions they've made and formally killed**, "including the ones you formally killed and why" (trade-off memory).
- Their **AI P&L assumptions** (model tiering / routing preferences, spend thresholds).
- Their **agent-led / assisted / never** calls per function, with reasoning attached.

### Features the app should make effortless
- A **morning briefing** that turns "120 emails of noise into a clear top-three by nine," tuned so "the urgent stops crowding out the important."
- **Monitoring that reads real output, not status reports** — never "just reporting green." Same-hour failure alerts.
- **Before-and-after testing** of a prompt with vs. without context (the in-room "feel the difference" moment).
- The **board one-pager / decision memo** generator — "what AI costs you, what it returns, what you are recommending."
- An **autonomy line** control surface: what the AI does alone / drafts / never touches; approval gates on anything "external, expensive, or irreversible."

### Tone / positioning the app should carry
Anti-hype, receipts-first, scars-included. CTRL should never overclaim or "report green." It should feel like it was built by an operator who runs the thing — confident, concrete, allergic to abstraction and theatre.
