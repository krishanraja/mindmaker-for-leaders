# The live session

Ninety minutes, one person, their own work in front of them. This is the highest-yield instrument available and it only works if the preparation happened.

## The precondition, and it is hard

**Before the session they send 10 to 14 real pieces of their own past work, split into two piles: would sign this off today, and would not.** No explanation, no annotation. Just two piles.

If they arrive without the piles, reschedule. Do not run this on description alone. Asking an expert what makes good work produces a plausible, generic, useless answer, because the knowledge is not consciously available on demand. It only surfaces against specific artefacts. Without the piles you have a conversation, not an elicitation, and you will write the rubric yourself and attribute it to them.

**Reserve the held-out set on day one.** Take ten pieces out of their piles at random before you look at anything, and never show them in the session. That is the only honest measurement that will ever exist. Reserve them on day one or there is no measurement, only usage.

## Do not send an agenda

An agenda invites them to prepare a theory of their own method. The whole point of the instrument is to bypass that. Concurrent verbalisation while looking at something does not alter how someone thinks. Asking them to explain, justify or introspect produces a reconstruction, and the reconstruction is contaminated.

## The opening, say roughly this

> I am not going to ask you about your method. I am going to show you your own work in threes and ask which two go together. That is the whole session. It will feel trivial and it is not.

## Minutes 0 to 25: triads

Lay out three pieces. Ask:

> How are two of these similar and the third one different?

If they stall:

> Pick the two that are most similar and tell me why. Then tell me how the third one is different.

Write their words down exactly. Do not paraphrase into yours. "It has actually seen the client" is data. "Client specificity" is your gloss and it is worth less.

Then the move the whole session turns on:

> And what would you call the opposite of that? Not the dictionary opposite. The opposite as you mean it.

That gap is the tacit judgment you are hunting. Present may contrast with performing, not with absent. Careful may contrast with slow, not with careless. A construct with a dictionary antonym as its contrast pole has not been elicited, it has been guessed, and the next stage will reject it.

Expect 6 to 9 constructs in 25 minutes, 8 to 15 across the session. If two constructs match above roughly 80 percent, they are one construct and you will merge them later.

Fallbacks when a triad stalls: compare two and ask for a contrasting third, or describe one in a single word and ask for its opposite.

## Minutes 25 to 36: rate

Take the three constructs they had the most energy about. Rate all the pieces 1 to 5 on each, anchored on the emergent pole, direction consistent.

This is the step people skip and it is the step that makes the output analysable rather than anecdotal. Without ratings you have anecdotes about work. With ratings you have a grid the next stage can run a discrimination test against.

## Minutes 36 to 50: ladder

Top two constructs only, both directions.

**Upward, for the reason.** "Why is this important to you? What does it mean to you?" Repeat three times. Stop when they start repeating themselves. This produces the rationale text, and the rationale is what makes a rule extendable rather than arbitrary.

**Downward, for the observable.** "How would you do that? What would that look like on the page?" Then the one that matters most:

> If I handed you a new one, what would I be able to point at that tells you it is there? What would I point at that tells you it is missing?

That answer is the criterion. Everything above it was scaffolding.

If a ladder blocks, do not press. Note the question and come back to it, or invert and ask for reasons they would not do something.

## Minutes 50 to 70: the reject pile

Some people state their standard only in the negative. If you have one of those, or if you have time left, switch instruments.

Put 30 to 40 pieces in front of them, mixed: plausible mediocre work and pieces they consider genuinely good, unlabelled and shuffled. The script is three sentences:

> Yes or no. One line why. Do not write anything better.

About 40 seconds an item. They write nothing except the one line. **The one line is the most valuable output of the entire session**, more valuable than the verdict, because the verdict tells you what and the line tells you why.

Say this before you start, and mean it:

> Most of these are deliberately mediocre. Rejecting a lot of them is the normal result and it is the useful one.

That preamble is not politeness. Pre-normalising the undesirable answer is the highest-efficacy debiasing move measured across 121 experiments, and without it you get the polite answer.

## Minutes 70 to 90: gaps only

If constructs are still thin, ask for six specific incidents. Not the method, specific rooms on specific days. Then probe:

- What were you seeing or hearing at that moment?
- Were you reminded of any previous experience?
- What rule were you following?
- How might someone new have got this wrong?

## Never, in a session

- Never ask them to rate their own confidence at anything.
- Never ask about learning style or personality type. Learning styles are dead at an effect size of 0.04 and personality matching has no controlled evidence of improving any outcome.
- Never ask them to describe their working style in the abstract. People are poor witnesses to their own habits.
- Never let an AI run the elicitation. Models elicit less than half of implicit requirements and are worst at exactly the style-related dimensions this session exists to surface. Use the AI to run the grading, not the eliciting.
- Never end without the next thing scheduled.

## Budget, honestly

Eight to twelve hours across three sessions plus your analysis time, for someone whose judgment other people rely on. Three to four hours for someone whose standard is narrower. Anyone quoting less is skipping the elicitation and writing the rubric themselves.

## Writing it up

One row per construct in `constructs.md`: emergent pole in their words, contrast pole in their words, the why-ladder chain, the how-ladder observables, and which triad it came from. One row per graded piece in `grades.jsonl`. Nothing else.

Do not write a summary document.
